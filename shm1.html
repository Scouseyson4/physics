SIMPLE HARMONIC MOTION


Among all the motions we study in high school physics, simple harmonic is the most important one to study. This is bacause of the fact that this type motion is exhibited by waves which we mostly study in high school physics.Both light and sound waves exhibit simple harmonic motion, so its important to study this motion properly.

Basically, a body exhibiting simple harmonic motion has two characterisitics. They are periodicity and oscillation.

If a body in motion exhibits peiodicity (repeats its motion after certain interval of time) and moves back and forth about a fix point (equilibrium) then it is in simple harmonic motion. Examples of it are: Motion of pendulum, Motion of spring mass system etc.

Likewise, in mathematical terms SHM can be described as "The motion in which restoring force applied on body is equal to its displacement but acts in opposite direction of its acceleration". i.e
F∝x ( As force is in opposite direction )
or, F=-kx ( k is constant of proportanility )
or, d²x/dt²=-kx ( In terms of differential equation )
where x is the position of body from equilibrium position.-ve sign comes as the froce acts in opposite direction of body's motion.

Now we are going to derive some important physical quantities (position, velocity, acceleration etc) of a body exhibiting SHM.

DERIVATIONS


For deriving the equations of simple harmonic motion, we can take the help of a body moving in circular motion. As SHM is a projection of a circular motion, it will be easy for us to derive the equations.

Now, Let's consider a object moving in a uniform circular motion along a circle of radius'r'. Suppose, the object is at A initially and after time't' it moves to point P. Let 'θ' be the angle covered. Now, when object reaches point P, we can draw two perpendicular lines PM and Pn on both X & Y axis. These points, M and N, then exhibit SHM along the axis or diameter of the circle.

Now for calculating the displacement of these points from O we can use trigonometry. Let 'y' be PM i.e displacement of M along X-axis. In triangle POM we can see,
sinθ= y/r
or, y=rsinθ
or, y=rsin(wt);-------(i)
w is angular velocity of object.
This is the equation for displacement of simple harmonic object. Here, radius 'r' is also the amplitude of the points, as 'r' is the highest displacement they can achieve from O.

Similarly, we can calculate the velocity of the point M moving in shm. For this we can take first derivative of displacement quation. i.e
dy/dt=d(rsin(wt))/dt
or, v=rwcos(wt)
or, v=rw√(1-sin^2(wt))
or, v=rw√(1-(y/r)^2)
or, v=w√(r^2-y^2)--------(ii)
